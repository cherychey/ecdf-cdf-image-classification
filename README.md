# Seven Numbers Suffice: Low-Dimensional ECDF Colour Descriptors for Image Clustering

Code and data for the paper submitted to *Signal, Image and Video Processing*.

Each image is represented by seven numbers: the values of its per-channel
empirical cumulative distribution function at intensity levels 100, 150 and
200, computed on a 32x32 resize. On 4400 rose images in four colour classes
this descriptor supports 100% five-fold cross-validated accuracy with a linear
SVM, a compression of 439:1 relative to the resized pixel array.

## What is here

```
notebooks/reproduce_all.ipynb   every table in the paper, end to end
data/descriptors/               extracted descriptors (7 columns per image)
results/                        the tables as produced by the notebook
paper/                          LaTeX source and bibliography
```

## What is not here, and why

The image collections are not redistributed. Both are publicly available from
their original sources, and the notebook downloads them:

- **Rose-4400** from Kaggle: `shuvokumarbasak2030/rose-color-classification-new-and-update-dataset`
- **Flowers102** from the Visual Geometry Group, University of Oxford

Redistributing them here would add hundreds of megabytes and would put us in
the position of relicensing data we do not own.

The intermediate spreadsheets holding raw pixel values are also omitted; they
run to tens of megabytes and are fully regenerated by the notebook. What is
committed instead is `data/descriptors/`, the seven-dimensional descriptors
themselves, which are small enough to version and are sufficient to reproduce
every number in the paper without touching an image.

## Reproducing the results

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
jupyter notebook notebooks/reproduce_all.ipynb
```

Run the cells in order. Sections 1 to 9 reproduce the Rose-4400 results from
the committed descriptors. Sections 10 and 11 download both collections and
rebuild the descriptors from the original images.

Downloading Rose-4400 requires Kaggle credentials. Place `kaggle.json` in
`~/.kaggle/` (`C:\Users\<name>\.kaggle\` on Windows). Do not commit that file;
`.gitignore` excludes it.

Section 8 extracts ResNet-50 features and needs `torch` and `torchvision`.
Every other section runs without them.

### Reproducibility of the extraction

We re-downloaded Rose-4400 from Kaggle and re-ran the extraction pipeline. For
each of the four classes the resulting set of descriptors is identical to ours
to machine precision; the two extractions differ only in file naming. The check
is in section 11.4 of the notebook and can be re-run.

`K`-means is sensitive to initialisation, so all results are reported over 100
random seeds as mean, standard deviation and percentile interval. Single-run
figures are not comparable to ours.

## Notes on the Flowers102 subset

The five colour categories are not human annotations. They were generated
automatically: dominant colour by `K`-means on a 100x100 version of each image,
then thresholds on hue and saturation. Two properties of that rule matter and
are documented in the paper: the hue ranges for blue and purple overlap, so
pure purple is labelled blue; and the brown category requires a conjunction of
conditions rarely satisfied, which is why it holds 21 images rather than 40.

The subset serves as a negative control, not as an independent benchmark. A
diagnostic cell in the notebook demonstrates both failures on synthetic
colours.
